Blockies / Concepts

Space and appearance

Blockies is a visual medium, so position matters to what you see. It matters to nothing else.

Running example

In the dashboard, the Composite sits at (100, 50) and each member has a local coordinate inside it. Moving the dashboard moves every member on screen without changing a single member coordinate.

World, Frame, placement

A World has exactly one Frame. The Frame is the root coordinate scope: everything ultimately resolves to a position inside it.

Placement is hierarchical and local. An entity's position is relative to its nearest placed ancestor: its Composite if it has one, otherwise the Frame. Moving a Composite moves everything inside it without touching a single member coordinate.

The analogy is a room number inside a building. Room 204 is on the second floor of a particular building. If the building is moved to another street, room 204 is still room 204; only its world position changes. That is why a Composite's transform is the only thing that changes when the group moves.

The transform

A placement is a translation plus a uniform scale. No rotation, and no non-uniform scale.

A concrete composition:

EntityLocal placementWorld position
dashboardtranslate (100, 50), scale 1(100, 50)
counter 1translate (20, 10), scale 1(120, 60)
gaugetranslate (240, 10), scale 1(340, 60)

Move the dashboard to (300, 200) and counter 1 moves to (320, 210) without its own placement changing. Scale the dashboard by 2 and counter 1's world position becomes (100 + 2×20, 50 + 2×10) = (140, 70); its size doubles too.

Why no rotation

Rotation composed with non-uniform scale is not closed: rotating a stretched object and then moving it into a differently stretched scope cannot always be expressed as one placement. Rather than have reparenting silently deform things, rotation is simply not in the model yet.

Placement is not membership

Three separate facts, again:

FactQuestionDashboard example
MembershipWhich Composite directly contains it?counter 1 is a member of dashboard
PlacementWhere is it, relative to its scope?counter 1 is at (20, 10) inside dashboard
NestingWhich Composite contains another?a "sales" Composite inside dashboard

Two Blockies can overlap on screen and be in different Composites. A member can be placed on the other side of the Frame. Dragging two Blockies together does not group them, and putting a Blockie next to a group does not make it the manager.

Placed, unplaced, backstage

An entity may have no placement record. That is different from a placement whose value is null — the protocol keeps absent and null distinct everywhere.

StatePlacement record?Drawn?Hit-tested?Why

Unplaced means: no coordinate, not drawn, not clickable, contributes nothing to any footprint. It still exists, it is still a member if it belongs to something, and it can still be counted and promoted.

The cascade rule

An unplaced Composite has no origin, so its members cannot resolve to world coordinates. They are not drawn, even if they have placement records of their own. To show a member, place its scope first.

Draw order

  • A Composite's body and chrome draw beneath its members.
  • Among members of one scope, order is the placement's layer, then the entity id.

Layer is presentation data. It never affects behaviour.

Footprint

A Composite's footprint is derived from where its members actually are. It is not stored, and it does not decide membership. A Composite can be spatially disconnected, with gaps, and that means nothing.

The dashboard's footprint is the union of the button, the counters, and the gauge. Move the gauge far away and the footprint grows; the membership list does not change.

The rule that keeps it honest

Geometry is semantically inert

Placement may change what you see. It may never change what happens. Randomising every coordinate in a World must leave its execution trace identical.

This is why "drag two Blockies together" does not make them a Composite, "put a supervisor next to a group" does not make it a manager, and reordering members in space does not reorder behaviour.

What draws a Composite?

A Blockie's appearance comes from its Blueprint. A Composite has no Blueprint, so it carries an optional presentation record instead:

FieldMeaningDashboard example
labelA human name, shown in chrome and inspectors."Sales dashboard"
rendererA renderer package — drawing only, not a Blueprint.the default bounds renderer
bodyAn optional member that visually stands for the assembly.the gauge

With no presentation record, the session's default composite renderer draws bounds and the id. That default has to look reasonable, because most Composites will have nothing else.

Three rules about appearance

  • Chrome is never interactive. Only Blockies are clickable. A decorative surface has no owner, so no authority could attach to it.
  • A Composite's own appearance is static. A Run never writes it. If it needs to change, a member changes.
  • Style is not in the document. Typography, materials, corner radii belong to the renderer package.

Views are cameras

There is one World and one placement tree. A view is a camera framing a region, plus a renderer, plus a grant scope. Views do not copy entities and do not reflow the layout.

That is why a presenter screen and an audience phone can show different parts of the same World without duplicating anything. The presenter's camera can include the button; the audience's can frame the gauge.

Common mistakes

  1. Inferring membership from position. Overlap is not containment. Membership is an explicit edge.
  2. Reparenting without preserving the world transform. C-22 requires the entity to stay where it appears; the local placement must be rewritten.
  3. Treating unplaced as null. Absence is a distinct state: the entity exists and can be promoted, but it has no coordinate.
  4. Drawing members of an unplaced Composite. No origin means no world position; the whole subtree is not drawn.
  5. Adding rotation or non-uniform scale to 0.1. The transform is closed only for translation plus uniform scale.

Check yourself

You move a Composite two cells right. What changes?

Only the Composite's transform. Member local coordinates are untouched, and no identity, state, or membership changes.

A member has a placement but is not drawn. Why?

Its Composite is unplaced, so there is no origin to resolve against. Placing the scope first brings the member back.

Why can't clicking a Composite's surface fire an event?

Because the surface has no owner and no ports. Interaction must resolve to a Blockie's port or to a Composite alias that points at one.

Two Blockies overlap on screen. Are they members of the same Composite?

Not necessarily. Membership is an explicit edge; position never implies it. They can overlap and belong to different Composites, or to none.

What does "geometry is semantically inert" forbid?

Any behaviour that depends on coordinates: inferring membership from proximity, ordering execution by position, or using placement to decide authority. Randomising every coordinate must leave the trace unchanged.
