Blockies / Concepts

Composites

A Composite is a multi-cell widget: one Brain, zero or more Parts, and zero or more Members. It is not a loose group of Blockies; it is a thing made of cells.

Running example

The gauge is a Composite of three cells: a Brain and two segments. The dashboard is a Composite of five children: a Brain and four Members, one of which is the gauge Composite. The difference is the role of the children, not the kind of parent.

The gauge: a Brain and its Parts

The gauge is one visual object, but it is made of three Blockies. A single Blockie cannot change its width or height, so a three-cell gauge needs three cells. The Brain owns the behavior; the Parts render the segments. The Parts' index configs live on the Blockies, not on the Composite; the Composite only holds the role edges.

composite gaugeWidget
  brain: gaugeBrain
  parts:
    gaugeSegment0
    gaugeSegment1

  aliases:
    count    -> gaugeBrain.count
    display  -> gaugeBrain.display

  connections:
    gaugeBrain.level -> gaugeSegment0.level
    gaugeBrain.level -> gaugeSegment1.level

The dashboard: a Brain and its Members

The dashboard is a larger Composite. Its Brain is the manager Blockie; its Members are independent widgets. The gauge is a Member, not a Part, because it has its own Brain and its own behavior.

composite dashboard
  brain: managerBlockie
  members:
    button       { role: member }
    counter1     { role: member }
    counter2     { role: member }
    gaugeWidget  { role: member }

  aliases:
    press    -> button.on_press
    count    -> counter1.count
    display  -> gaugeWidget.display
    status   -> managerBlockie.status

  fold total = sum(counter1.count, counter2.count)
  manager: orchestrator, hosted by managerBlockie

The four roles

Brain is not Manager

The Brain is a Blockie role: it owns the Composite's behavior. The Manager is a Run role: it coordinates Members. A Brain may host the Manager Run, but being the Brain does not grant management authority. In the gauge, the Brain is compute-only and there is no Manager at all.

Membership rules

  • Exactly one Brain. It must be a direct member and a Blockie. The Composite references the Brain's identity, never its Blueprint.
  • At least one non-Brain child. A Composite with only a Brain is a Blockie, not a Composite.
  • Parts are Blockies and must be vacant. If a Part hosts a Run, its role is member, not part.
  • Members may be Blockies or Composites. A nested Composite is always a Member, never a Part.
  • One parent. A child is a direct member of at most one Composite.
  • Acyclic. Containment cannot loop.
  • Non-destructive. Removing a child does not delete it. Removing the last non-Brain child dissolves the Composite; the Brain becomes standalone.

Aliases: the public face

A Composite has no ports. It has a table of aliases, each pointing at the Brain's port, a Member's port, or a fold. An alias must never source a Part.

aliases:
  count    -> gaugeBrain.count
  display  -> gaugeBrain.display
  press    -> button.on_press
  total    -> fold(sum, [counter1.count, counter2.count])

Alias vs connection

Aliases chain through nesting. The dashboard's display alias points at gaugeWidget.display, which points at gaugeBrain.display. External consumers never see the gauge's parts.

Folds: declared aggregation

A normal value input accepts one producer, so SUM(A1:A10) is impossible. A fold is the declared exception: a Composite may aggregate a set of Member outputs with one operation — sum, count, min, max, collect. It never selects a Part.

fold total:
  operation: sum
  selects:   [counter1.count, counter2.count]
  result:    integer

The fold is revalidated on every membership change: every selected Member must still expose the declared port with a compatible type.

The Manager

A Composite may designate zero or one Manager. The Manager is a Run hosted by a Brain or Member — never a Part — and it must be occupied.

CanCannot
dispatch an event to a Member or the Brain in memberSetdispatch to a Part
read its own reads valuesread child Blockie state or configuration
publish its own process outputsadd, remove, move, or reparent children
be observed through an aliascancel a child's Run
—write any part of the document

When the Manager's Run becomes terminal, five effects commit in one generation: the Run status, the host's vacancy, the cleared designation, the revoked grant, and the resulting output unavailability. If the Manager dies, the Brain and the children remain; only the designation is cleared.

Nesting

The gauge is a Member of the dashboard. The dashboard sees the gauge's aliases (count, display) and nothing else. It cannot address the gauge's Brain or Parts directly. The gauge has its own Brain and no Manager; the dashboard's Manager gains no authority over it.

Nesting is what makes the tree recursive: a dashboard can be a Member of an app Composite; a gauge can be a Member of a dashboard; a Part is always a leaf Blockie.

Common mistakes

  1. Treating a Composite as a loose group. It has a Brain; that is what makes it a thing rather than a set.
  2. Treating the Brain as the Manager. The Brain owns behavior; the Manager is a Run that coordinates Members.
  3. Treating a Part as a Member. A Part is a visual extension and must be vacant; a Member is independent and may host a Run.
  4. Making a Part an alias source or a dispatch target. Parts are internal; the Brain drives them.
  5. Removing the Brain without replacement. Replace it or dissolve the Composite; a Brain-only Composite is a Blockie.
  6. Forgetting the non-Brain child. A Composite needs at least two cells: the Brain and at least one Part or Member.

Check yourself

How many cells does the gauge have?

Three: one Brain and two Parts.

Why is the gauge a Composite and not a Blockie?

Because it spans three cells and has a Brain plus Parts. A Blockie is one cell.

Why is the dashboard a Composite?

Because it has a Brain (the manager Blockie), Members (the widgets), an interface, and a footprint. It is a larger widget, not a loose group.

Can the dashboard's Manager dispatch to a gauge segment?

No. The segments are Parts, not Members, and Parts are never in the memberSet.

What happens when the last non-Brain child is removed?

The Composite dissolves; the Brain becomes a standalone Blockie. A Brain-only Composite is not a Composite.

Is the Manager the same as the Brain?

No. The Brain is a Blockie role; the Manager is a Run role. They may be hosted by the same Blockie, but they are different facts.
